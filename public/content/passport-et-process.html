Passport ET began with a very ordinary frustration: people needed to know whether their passport was ready, where to pick it up, and which day made sense to go. The information existed, but it lived in formats and workflows that were not exactly designed for an anxious applicant checking from a phone, a family member helping from abroad, or someone trying to avoid one more unnecessary trip across town. In other words, the data was technically public; the experience was politely hiding behind a pile of PDFs.

We turned that gap into a public-interest product. Passport ET lets users search by request number, name, or related fields, browse the latest passports ready for pickup, and open detailed readiness information that explains pickup location and timing. The first version focused on the essential civic workflow: make the answer findable, make the result understandable, and make the system reliable enough that people would return when new publications were released.


The backend was built fully in the open at https://github.com/NegusNati/passport.et under the MIT License. The repository describes a Laravel/containerized system with role and permission based admin routes, PDF-to-database parsing, queued background jobs for parsing work, automated daily updates, rate limiting, Chapa payment integration, Docker Compose, Traefik deployment, and production-oriented operational notes. It was not a closed consultancy artifact dropped over a wall. It was public code, public learning, public accountability, and a small invitation for other developers to inspect, reuse, and improve the work.

The newer frontend lives at https://github.com/NegusNati/passport-frontend and carries the modern product experience. It uses a React/TanStack foundation with a search-first interface, Tailwind-based UI, Web Vitals and PostHog tracking, performance testing, and a cleaner interaction model for people who just want a straight answer. That split let us keep the data engine serious while giving the public interface the polish, speed, and calm that a high-stress civic task deserves.

Around eight months ago, the rebrand and UI/UX refresh changed the trajectory. The product moved from useful tool to recognizable public utility. The refreshed interface improved trust at first glance, made mobile scanning easier, clarified result states, reduced friction in repeat checks, and gave the whole experience a more official, less improvised feel. Same mission, sharper suit, fewer confused taps.


The usage data reflects that shift. Passport ET has now surpassed more than 1.9 million recorded passport entries. More than 140,000 people have used it since launch, and the product is still growing, averaging around 12,000 users per month today. In the latest 30-day analytics snapshot, Passport ET recorded 11.8k visitors, 12.1k visits, 35.2k views, a 45% bounce rate, and an average visit duration of 1m 58s. The audience is strongly Ethiopian, with meaningful diaspora and regional usage from Somalia, the United States, South Africa, the Netherlands, the UAE, France, Saudi Arabia, the United Kingdom, and Germany.

What we built is more than a passport search page. It is an open-source civic product with a data pipeline, admin tooling, a modern public frontend, analytics, performance awareness, and a brand system strong enough to become memorable. The result is a service that quietly does something important: it gives people back time, certainty, and a little bit of dignity in a process that usually asks them to bring their own patience.
