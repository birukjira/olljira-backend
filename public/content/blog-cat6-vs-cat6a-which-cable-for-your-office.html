"Should we use Cat6 or Cat6A?" is one of the most common questions in our site surveys — and the honest answer is that it depends on three things: distance, bandwidth plans, and how painful it would be to re-cable later. What the specs actually say Cat6 carries 1 Gbps to 100 meters and 10 Gbps to about 55 meters. Cat6A carries 10 Gbps the full 100 meters, with better shielding against interference. Cat6A cable is thicker, less flexible, and roughly 30–50% more expensive per meter, and it needs slightly larger containment. When Cat6 is enough For most offices under 50 meters per run, with typical workloads — browsing, VoIP, shared drives, cloud apps — Cat6 delivers everything you will use for years. If your runs are short and your internet connection is under 1 Gbps, Cat6A buys you headroom you may never touch. When Cat6A is worth it New construction or major renovation, where walls will be closed for a decade or more. Runs longer than 55 meters where you want 10-gig capability. Dense environments with lots of cable bundled together, where Cat6A's shielding earns its keep. And anywhere you expect to upgrade switches to multi-gig within the cable's lifetime. The question that actually decides it Cabling outlives every device attached to it. Switches get replaced in 5 years; cable stays for 15. So ask: what would it cost to re-cable this space later? If the answer involves closed walls, operating hotel floors, or a working clinic, the extra cost of Cat6A today is cheap insurance. Whatever you choose, every run gets tested with a Fluke analyzer and the printed results go in your handover folder. The test report matters more than the category on the box.
