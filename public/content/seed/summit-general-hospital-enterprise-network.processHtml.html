Summit General Hospital runs on its network — patient records, lab systems, and internal communications all depend on it. The hospital needed a nine-floor enterprise network built to a standard where failure is not an option, and it had to happen while the hospital stayed fully operational. Backbone and data center We designed a fiber uplink backbone connecting all nine floors to a climate-controlled 42U data center on the main floor. Every floor got its own distribution point, cabled and labeled back to the core, so a fault on one floor never touches the rest of the building. Systems on top of the network On top of the physical network we deployed Active Directory for centralized user management, a PBX phone system for internal communication, and door access control for restricted areas like pharmacies and records rooms. The network was integrated with the hospital's HIMS management system so clinical staff authenticate once and work everywhere. Surveillance and alerts 32 CCTV cameras cover entrances, corridors, and critical areas, paired with motion sensors that raise smart alerts instead of endless false alarms. Retention and remote access were configured to the hospital administration's policy, with full documentation handed over to their IT team.
