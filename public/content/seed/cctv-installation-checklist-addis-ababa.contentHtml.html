Most CCTV disappointments are decided before a single camera is mounted. The camera count was guessed, the storage was undersized, or nobody checked the lighting at 2 AM. After designing systems for hotels, clinics, schools, and offices across Addis Ababa, this is the checklist we walk through on every site survey — and the one you should use to evaluate any installer, including us. 1. Map your coverage zones first Walk the property and list what you actually need to see: entrances and exits, corridors, cash handling points, server rooms, parking, and perimeters. A corridor camera and a gate camera are different products. If an installer quotes you a camera count before walking the site, the design is a guess. 2. Check lighting at night, not just at noon Cameras that look fine in daylight can be useless after dark. Note where IR illumination will be needed, where headlights or floodlights will blow out the image, and where you may need low-light (ColorVu-class) cameras instead of standard ones. 3. Plan power and pathways Where does each camera's cable physically run? Is there existing containment? Where is the nearest power for the NVR and switches? In Addis, also plan for power cuts — a small UPS for the NVR keeps recording through outages that would otherwise leave gaps exactly when you need footage most. 4. Size storage for retention, not for the sales brochure Decide how many days of footage you need — we recommend 30 days minimum for businesses — and size the NVR storage from real bitrate math, not "supports up to X cameras." Ask the installer to show the calculation. 5. Demand remote viewing setup and admin handover Remote viewing on your phone should be configured and tested with you before the crew leaves. Admin passwords should be handed to you in writing, not kept by the installer. 6. Get it in writing Camera models, lens types, storage size, retention math, warranty terms — all of it belongs in the quote, line by line. A one-year workmanship warranty should cover anything the installation itself got wrong. A camera system you can trust is designed, not purchased.
